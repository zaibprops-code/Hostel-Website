Choosing where to live in a new city is one of the biggest decisions you'll
make as a student or young professional. Get it right and everything else gets
easier. Here's a grounded checklist we'd give our own younger siblings.

## 1. Start with safety

Before anything else, ask how security actually works — not whether it "has
security," but the specifics:

- Is there a **staffed front desk**, and is it staffed at night?
- Is entry through a **single, monitored point**?
- Is there **CCTV** on entrances and shared corridors?
- Are visitors **signed in**?

If the answers are vague, keep looking.

## 2. Check the things you'll use every day

The glossy photos matter less than the boring essentials:

- **Internet** that holds up during online classes and deadlines
- **Backup power** for load-shedding
- **Hot water** in the morning
- **Clean washrooms** — ask to see them, not just the bedroom

## 3. Understand the true cost

Ask for the **total** monthly cost, then confirm what's included. A low
headline rent with utilities, internet and "service charges" added later can
cost more than an all-inclusive rate. A trustworthy hostel will give you a
clear breakdown and a receipt.

> Transparency is itself a trust signal. If a place is cagey about money before
> you move in, imagine dealing with it afterwards.

## 4. Visit — or take a virtual tour

Photos can hide a lot. Visit in person if you can. If you can't, ask for a
**video walkthrough** of the actual room and common areas, not a marketing
reel.

## 5. Location, realistically

Measure the commute to your campus or office **at the time you'll actually
travel**, and check what's within walking distance: food, a pharmacy, an ATM, a
bus stop.

## A quick decision framework

If a hostel is **safe**, **clean**, **reliable on the essentials**, **honest
about pricing**, and **well-located** — you've found a good one. Everything else
is a bonus.
