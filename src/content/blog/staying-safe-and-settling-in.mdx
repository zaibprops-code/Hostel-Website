A good hostel keeps you safe; good habits keep you safer. And feeling *at home*
is its own kind of security. Here's how to do both.

## Safety habits worth keeping

- **Lock your storage**, even for short trips out. It's a two-second habit.
- **Know the exits** and where the emergency contacts are posted.
- **Sign your guests in.** The visitor policy protects you too.
- **Keep emergency numbers saved:** the front desk, Rescue 1122, Police 15.

At Riwaq these are backed by a 24/7 staffed desk, a single monitored entrance
and CCTV — but your own habits are the first layer.

## Settling in quickly

The first week is the hardest. A few things help:

- **Say hello.** The people in your common lounge are about to become friends.
- **Set a small routine** — a study spot, a gym time, a call home.
- **Ask the staff.** In a well-run hostel, the manager genuinely wants to help.

## Look after yourself

- Keep a little emergency cash and a basic medicine kit.
- Register with the front desk who your **emergency contact** is.
- Don't power through feeling unwell — tell someone.

## Home is a feeling, not just a place

The best hostels are the ones where, after a few weeks, you stop thinking of it
as "the hostel" and start calling it "home." That's the bar we aim for.
