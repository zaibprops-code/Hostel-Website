Living away from home for the first time means managing money for the first
time too. Here's a straightforward way to think about a monthly student budget
in Islamabad — no spreadsheets-from-hell required.

## The 50/30/20 idea, student edition

A simple rule to start with:

- **~50% — essentials:** accommodation, food, transport
- **~30% — studies & living:** books, data, the occasional outing
- **~20% — savings & buffer:** for emergencies and the unexpected

Your numbers will vary, but having *categories* stops money from vanishing.

## Where the money actually goes

The big three for most students are **rent, food, and transport**. A useful
move is to bundle as much as possible into predictable, fixed costs so you're
not nickel-and-dimed by surprises.

This is where **all-inclusive hostel living helps**: when WiFi, electricity,
water and housekeeping are part of one monthly rent, your single largest
category becomes a fixed, known number. No shock utility bills.

## Small habits that add up

- **Cook sometimes.** A shared kitchen turns a few home-cooked meals a week
  into real savings over a semester.
- **Split and share.** Groceries and rides are cheaper shared with the people
  you already live with.
- **Track for one month.** Just noting what you spend for 30 days is eye-opening.

## Build a tiny buffer

Even PKR 1,000–2,000 set aside each month becomes a cushion that means a broken
laptop charger or a medical co-pay isn't a crisis.
